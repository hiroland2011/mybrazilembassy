**2026 Presidential Elections**

On October 4, the first round of elections took place, but without a candidate reaching a majority, leading to a second round of elections to determine a majority government. The main candidates are Flávio Bolsonaro, representing the Liberal Party (far-right), and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, representing the Workers' Party (PT). Flávio obtained about 47% of the votes while Lula received about 45%, leaving both under the 50% threshold. People who voted for Flávio stated that their reasoning was that the PT had accomplished nothing during the 20 non-consecutive years it spent in power. Meanwhile, throughout his time as president of the country, Lula has been noted for his work with social programs that have helped millions of people living below the poverty line since the 2000s. On the other hand, Flávio's supporters believe that the cost of living as well as the crime rate increased while Lula was in power. One of Flávio's promises is to solve these problems, lower taxes, end political persecution, amend the Brazilian constitution, and ultimately liberate the Brazilian people. Those who voted for Lula argue that voting for Bolsonaro is equivalent to voting for barbarism, that they want sovereignty, and that Flávio wants to sell Brazil to the United States. Other reasons include concern for the environment, specifically the deforestation of the Amazon, which decreased significantly during Lula's term. The same voters who supported Lula also consider Flávio to be involved in an ongoing corruption scandal, the Banco Master scandal.

**Violence Against Multiple Communities**

In Brazil, there is widespread violence that takes different forms and targets several groups of people, such as the LGBTQ+ community, Indigenous peoples, and Black people. Indigenous peoples normally have lands granted to them by the government. However, the lack of policies to protect them means they frequently face violence and threats of land grabbing. Human rights defenders are victims of attacks and threats, sometimes leading to their deaths. Police violence remains another major persistent issue. Brazil ranks among the countries with the highest homicide rates due to police violence, with the majority of victims being Black. Violence against women also remains a major problem. Support measures have been implemented to help, but the number of victims remains very high. Violence against all these groups continues to increase every year, and few measures are put in place by the government—those that are implemented are often not enforced. This drives the population to protest, calling on authorities to do more to address the problem.

**Banco Master Scandal**

In November 2025, Banco Master entered liquidation with losses amounting to 47.3 billion reais, or 1.3 billion CAD. Nearly 90% of the money was reimbursed to depositors/shareholders by the FGC. The bank created forged documents and fake loans to commit fraudulent acts tied to political corruption. Since 2018, Banco Master managed state and municipal pension funds as well as banking services. Banco Master therefore held a significant amount of public money. To keep the public blind to the bank's activities, Vorcaro, the owner of Banco Master, assembled a team of senior civil servants, economists, judges, influencers, and political figures using questionable methods according to prosecutors. However, this attempt to keep the public in the dark failed, and he turned to blackmail and physical threats against journalists and critics. Taking advantage of Brazil's unstable political situation throughout his career, he funneled money to the right wing, specifically Ciro Nogueira, leader of the right-wing coalition and former minister under Bolsonaro. Additionally, 2025 voice recordings between Flávio Bolsonaro, the right-wing candidate for the 2026 elections, and Vorcaro were uncovered, indicating that he received money to finance the film "Dark Horse," which focuses on the life of former president Jair Bolsonaro. Jaques Wagner, a left-wing senator on Lula's team, received an apartment in Salvador worth 3.5 million reais and facilitated Vorcaro's business as early as 2018. All this goes to show that, whether on the right or the left, corruption is rampant in Brazil. If Lula, who does not appear to be directly implicated in the scandal and has remained silent so far, wins the next election, he will have many issues to address. If Flávio Bolsonaro wins, the scandal risks fading away, though he could still face impeachment by the Supreme Court. The Banco Master scandal thus exerts a powerful influence on Brazilian politics and the upcoming elections.
