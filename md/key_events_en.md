# 20 Key Events in Brazilian History

### 1. 1500 — Arrival of Pedro Álvares Cabral
Portuguese explorer Pedro Álvares Cabral arrived on the coast of present-day Bahia, claiming the land for the Portuguese Crown. Initially named *Terra de Santa Cruz*, the territory later became known as Brazil due to the valuable brazilwood (*pau-brasil*) extracted along the coast.
* [Image 1: Landing of Cabral (Historical Painting)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2a/Desembarque_de_Pedro_%C3%81lvares_Cabral_em_Porto_Seguro_1500.jpg)
* [Image 2: Modern Monument of Cabral in Porto Seguro](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e4/Monumento_a_Cabral_Porto_Seguro.jpg)

### 2. 1532 — Establishment of São Vicente
Martim Afonso de Sousa founded São Vicente in present-day São Paulo state, marking the first permanent Portuguese settlement in Brazil. This established the system of hereditary captaincies, laying the administrative and institutional groundwork for Portuguese colonial colonization and sugar production.
* [Image 1: Historical Depiction of São Vicente Colony](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/86/Sao_Vicente_1532.jpg)
* [Image 2: Modern View of São Vicente Coastline](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/36/Praia_de_S%C3%A3o_Vicente_SP.jpg)

### 3. 1549 — Creation of the General Government and Salvador
King John III of Portugal centralized colonial administration by appointing Tomé de Sousa as Brazil's first Governor-General. De Sousa established Salvador, Bahia, as the colony's first capital, bringing Jesuit missionaries to convert Indigenous populations and organizing imperial governance.
* [Image 1: Historic Architecture in Salvador da Bahia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Pelourinho_Salvador_Bahia_Brazil.jpg)
* [Image 2: Statue of Tomé de Sousa in Salvador](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/Tom%C3%A9_de_Sousa_Salvador.jpg)

### 4. 1654 — Expulsion of the Dutch from Pernambuco
After decades of Dutch West India Company occupation in northeastern Brazil (New Holland), Portuguese colonists, Indigenous fighters, and Afro-Brazilian troops defeated the Dutch forces at the Battles of Guararapes, forcing their ultimate surrender and securing Portuguese territorial dominance.
* [Image 1: Battle of Guararapes Painting](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5e/Batalha_dos_Guararapes_-_Victor_Meirelles.jpg)
* [Image 2: Historic Fort of Orange in Pernambuco](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Forte_Orange_Itamaraca_Pernambuco.jpg)

### 5. 1695 — Death of Zumbi dos Palmares
Zumbi, the leader of Quilombo dos Palmares—the largest autonomous settlement of escaped enslaved Africans in Brazil—was captured and executed by colonial forces. His legacy made him an enduring national symbol of Afro-Brazilian resistance, freedom, and anti-slavery struggle.
* [Image 1: Monument to Zumbi dos Palmares in Rio de Janeiro](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Zumbi_dos_Palmares_monument_Rio_de_Janeiro.jpg)
* [Image 2: Symbolic Art of Zumbi dos Palmares](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/83/Zumbi_dos_Palmares_symbolic.jpg)

### 6. 1789 — The Inconfidência Mineira
A anti-colonial conspiracy led by Joaquim José da Silva Xavier ("Tiradentes") emerged in Minas Gerais, inspired by Enlightenment ideals and the American Revolution. Although the Portuguese Crown crushed the revolt and executed Tiradentes, it marked a crucial early milestone toward Brazilian independence.
* [Image 1: Execution of Tiradentes (Historical Painting)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/Tiradentes_esquartejado_-_Pedro_Am%C3%A9rico.jpg)
* [Image 2: Ouro Preto, Historical Center of Minas Gerais](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ouro_Preto_Minas_Gerais_Brazil.jpg)

### 7. 1808 — Arrival of the Portuguese Royal Family
Fleeing Napoleon’s invading armies, Prince Regent John (later King John VI) and the Portuguese court relocated to Rio de Janeiro. This transformed Rio into the seat of the Portuguese Empire, opened Brazilian ports to friendly nations, and elevated Brazil's political status globally.
* [Image 1: Arrival of the Royal Family in Bahia (Painting)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5c/Chegada_da_Familia_Real_Portuguesa_a_Bahia.jpg)
* [Image 2: Imperial Museum of Brazil in Petrópolis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/Museu_Imperial_Petropolis_Brazil.jpg)

### 8. 1822 — Declaration of Brazilian Independence
On September 7, Prince Pedro declared Brazil’s independence from Portugal along the Ipiranga River, uttering the famous cry "Independence or Death!" He was crowned Emperor Pedro I, establishing the Empire of Brazil as a constitutional monarchy distinct from neighboring Spanish-American republics.
* [Image 1: Independence or Death Painting by Pedro Américo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/Independ%C3%AAncia_ou_Morte_-_Pedro_Am%C3%A9rico.jpg)
* [Image 2: Monument to the Independence of Brazil in São Paulo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/44/Monumento_do_Ipiranga_Sao_Paulo.jpg)

### 9. 1831 — Abdication of Emperor Pedro I
Facing growing political opposition, economic instability, and dynastic turmoil in Portugal, Pedro I abdicated the Brazilian throne in favor of his five-year-old son, Pedro II. This initiated the tumultuous Regency Period, characterized by regional rebellions across the nation.
* [Image 1: Portrait of Emperor Pedro I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/22/Dom_Pedro_I_do_Brasil.jpg)
* [Image 2: Portrait of Young Emperor Pedro II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/62/Dom_Pedro_II_young.jpg)

### 10. 1864–1870 — The Paraguayan War
Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay formed the Triple Alliance to defeat Paraguayan dictator Francisco Solano López in South America's deadliest military conflict. The victory expanded Brazil's territorial border claims but caused heavy casualties, economic strain, and bolstered the political influence of the military.
* [Image 1: Battle of the Riachuelo (Naval Engagement)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/84/Batalha_do_Riachuelo_-_Victor_Meirelles.jpg)
* [Image 2: Brazilian Troops in the Paraguayan War](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Guerra_do_Paraguai_-_Tropas_brasileiras.jpg)

### 11. 1888 — Abolition of Slavery (Golden Law)
Princess Isabel signed the *Lei Áurea* (Golden Law) on May 13, unconditionally abolishing slavery in Brazil. As the last nation in the Western Hemisphere to eradicate chattel slavery, the event radically reshaped Brazilian social structures and accelerated the collapse of the monarchy.
* [Image 1: Princess Isabel of Brazil](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/91/Princesa_Isabel_do_Brasil_1880s.jpg)
* [Image 2: Proclamation of Abolition Painting](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9b/Abolicao_da_escravatura_-_A_Lei_Aurea.jpg)

### 12. 1889 — Proclamation of the Republic
Marshal Deodoro da Fonseca led a military coup that deposed Emperor Pedro II, ending the Empire of Brazil and establishing the United States of Brazil. The royal family was exiled, transitioning the country into a federal republic dominated by agrarian oligarchical elites.
* [Image 1: Marshal Deodoro da Fonseca](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/Marechal_Deodoro_da_Fonseca.jpg)
* [Image 2: Proclamation of the Republic Monument in Rio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bc/Praca_da_Republica_Rio_de_Janeiro.jpg)

### 13. 1930 — The Revolution of 1930
Getúlio Vargas led an armed revolution that ousted President Washington Luís and prevented the inauguration of Júlio Prestes, ending the "Coffee with Milk" oligarchy. Vargas assumed power, centralizing state authority, industrializing the economy, and establishing the influential *Estado Novo* regime in 1937.
* [Image 1: Getúlio Vargas Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4c/Getulio_Vargas_1930s.jpg)
* [Image 2: Constitutionalist Revolution Monument in São Paulo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9f/Obelisco_do_Ibirapuera_Sao_Paulo.jpg)

### 14. 1960 — Inauguration of Brasília
President Juscelino Kubitschek officially inaugurated Brasília as the nation's new federal capital, moving the government inland from Rio de Janeiro. Designed by Oscar Niemeyer and Lúcio Costa, the modernist city aimed to develop the interior and symbolize national modernization.
* [Image 1: National Congress of Brazil in Brasília](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Congresso_Nacional_do_Brasil_Brasilia.jpg)
* [Image 2: Cathedral of Brasília Architecture](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Catedral_de_Brasilia_interior_e_fachada.jpg)

### 15. 1964 — Military Coup d'État
The Brazilian Armed Forces, supported by conservative civil groups, overthrow Democratic President João Goulart. This established a authoritarian military dictatorship that lasted until 1985, characterized by political censorship, human rights abuses, economic expansion, and suppression of opposition.
* [Image 1: President João Goulart](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Joao_Goulart_1961.jpg)
* [Image 2: Military Junta Era Protest Symbol](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7b/Protest_against_dictatorship_Brazil.jpg)

### 16. 1984 — The *Diretas Já* Movement
Millions of Brazilians took to the streets across major cities in a massive civil movement demanding the immediate return of direct democratic presidential elections. While the constitutional amendment failed initially, the momentum effectively ended military rule and ushered in redemocratization.
* [Image 1: Diretas Já Rally in São Paulo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4e/Comicio_Diretas_Ja_Sao_Paulo.jpg)
* [Image 2: Banner from the Democratic Movement Era](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1a/Diretas_Ja_Protest.jpg)

### 17. 1988 — Enactment of the Citizen Constitution
Brazil promulgated a new Democratic Constitution, officially nicknamed the "Citizen Constitution." It restored civil liberties, established individual rights, created the Universal Health System (SUS), recognized Indigenous land rights, and laid the legal foundation for modern Brazilian democracy.
* [Image 1: National Congress Building where Constitution was signed](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Congresso_Nacional_do_Brasil_Brasilia.jpg)
* [Image 2: Modern Brazilian National Flag](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/05/Flag_of_Brazil.svg)

### 18. 1994 — Launch of the Plano Real
Finance Minister Fernando Henrique Cardoso introduced the *Plano Real*, a comprehensive economic stabilization program that created the Real (BRL) currency. The plan successfully halted hyperinflation that had plagued Brazil for decades, laying the groundwork for long-term economic stability.
* [Image 1: Fernando Henrique Cardoso](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bc/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso_official_presidential_portrait.jpg)
* [Image 2: Brazilian Real Currency Banknotes](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6f/Cedulas_do_Real.jpg)

### 19. 2014 — Launch of Operation Car Wash (*Lava Jato*)
Federal prosecutors launched a massive corruption investigation targeting money laundering, state oil company Petrobras, and major construction firms. The probe implicated dozens of high-ranking politicians and business executives, dramatically reshaping Brazilian politics and public trust.
* [Image 1: Petrobras Headquarters Building in Rio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/87/Edificio_Sede_da_Petrobras_Rio_de_Janeiro.jpg)
* [Image 2: Protests in Brazil during Operation Car Wash era](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Protestos_no_Brasil_em_2015.jpg)

### 20. 2016 — Impeachment of President Dilma Rousseff
Brazil’s Senate voted to remove Dilma Rousseff, the country's first female president, from office over allegations of budget law violations (*pedaladas fiscais*). The controversial impeachment ended 13 years of Workers' Party (PT) rule and deepened national political polarization.
* [Image 1: Dilma Rousseff Official Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Dilma_Rousseff_official_portrait_2011.jpg)
* [Image 2: National Congress during the Impeachment Debates](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8c/Congresso_Nacional_-_Brasilia_DF.jpg)
