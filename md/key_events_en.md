# 20 Key Events in Brazilian History

### 1. 1950 — Return of Getúlio Vargas to the Presidency
From 1930 to 1945, Getúlio Vargas served as President of Brazil representing the Workers' Party, but in 1945, he was overthrown by the military. He was re-elected as a senator for Rio Grande do Sul but did not return to national politics until 1950, when he became president again with the support of the Workers' Party. Swept up by corruption scandals and right-wing political campaigns, he committed suicide in 1954.

### 2. 1954 — Battle of Berne
In the 1954 World Cup quarter-finals, the Brazilian team played against Hungary with unusual violence due to their intense rivalry. Two players were sent off during the match, and at the end of the game, a massive brawl broke out between supporters of both teams, leaving several people injured.

### 3. 1958 — First World Cup Victory
Since participating in their first World Cup in 1930, the Brazilian men's national football team won its first title in 1958 in Sweden. The standout player of the tournament was Edson Arantes do Nascimento, also known as Pelé: although he was only 17 years old and injured, he completely dominated the game and captivated the crowds. Football immediately became deeply intertwined with Brazilian culture.

### 4. 1960 — Inauguration of Brasília
Juscelino Kubitschek launched a project to build a new capital, Brasília, to better distribute economic activities, wealth, and population inland. The project was also intended to end the rivalry between São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro. The city was almost entirely planned from scratch and remains a prime example of large-scale urban planning today.

### 5. 1964 — Beginning of the Military Dictatorship
João Goulart, a former Brazilian president, was accused by many—including sections of the public, the influential Church, and even the American press—of being a communist. During the coup d'état on April 1, the military met very little resistance because the population was accustomed to seeing the military manage state affairs. Under the dictatorship regime, severe censorship was imposed, and thousands of citizens were imprisoned, tortured, or killed.

### 6. 1970 — Launch of the Trans-Amazonian Highway Project
The Trans-Amazonian Highway project, launched by President Emílio Médici in 1970, aimed to connect the Amazon region with various other parts of Brazil. The project succeeded in attracting settlers to the Amazon. However, its construction raised major concerns as it caused widespread deforestation in the Amazon rainforest, which plays a crucial role in absorbing greenhouse gases.

### 7. 1976 — Assassination of Juscelino Kubitschek
The official version of the death of former president and dictatorship opponent Juscelino Kubitschek was a car accident. However, many believed he was assassinated by the military regime. In 2013, the São Paulo Truth Commission concluded based on evidence that Kubitschek's death was an assassination, though this finding was later refuted in 2014.

### 8. 1983-84 — The "Diretas Já" Movement
In 1983, during the dictatorship, the Brazilian public grew weary of the military regime due to an economic slowdown and the lack of direct presidential suffrage. They demanded a constitutional amendment to restore direct voting. Demonstrations featuring the slogan "Diretas Já" (Direct Elections Now) took place nationwide to improve living conditions and restore the right to choose the president directly.

### 9. 1985 — End of the Military Dictatorship
In 1979, an Amnesty Law was passed, granting immunity to authorities involved in state repression as well as to political opponents. A multi-party system was also re-established in 1979, paving the way for a transition to democracy. The first true civilian president after 1985 was José Sarney, as Tancredo Neves, who was elected to serve as president, died before taking office.

### 10. 1992 — "Caras-pintadas" Movement
Fernando Collor de Mello, the first democratically elected president following the dictatorship, was accused of corruption, including documented instances of tax evasion and illegal payments from construction companies. This sparked a student movement known as "Caras-pintadas" (Painted Faces), where youth painted their faces in the colors of the Brazilian flag. The movement was influential enough to prompt the Senate to initiate impeachment proceedings against the president.

### 11. October 1992 — Carandiru Massacre
On October 2, 1992, an altercation during a prisoner transfer led to a riot in the severely overcrowded Carandiru detention center in the state of São Paulo. Governor Luís Antônio Fleury Filho reportedly blocked negotiations with the rioting inmates and ordered a forceful storming of the prison. The Military Police, commanded by Colonel Ubiratan Guimarães, opened fire on the inmates, resulting in the deaths of 102 prisoners.

### 12. 1994 — Launch of the Plano Real
To curb runaway inflation, Finance Minister Fernando Henrique Cardoso introduced a new currency in 1994, the Real. The program successfully reduced annual inflation from over 5,000% to under 10% without stalling economic growth. The plan also reduced the proportion of Brazilians living in poverty from 33% to 25% within two years. Cardoso subsequently won the presidential election and served two consecutive terms.

### 13. 1995 — Creation of the ACTO
The Amazon Cooperation Treaty Organization (ACTO) was created to preserve the Amazon and its resources by addressing environmental issues, particularly climate change and deforestation in the "lungs of the Earth." In addition to Brazil, the organization includes several countries sharing the Amazon basin: Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, Guyana, Peru, Suriname, and Venezuela.

### 14. 2003 — First PT Administration Term
Representing the Workers' Party (PT), Lula launched comprehensive social programs to combat poverty and hunger, such as Fome Zero and Bolsa Família, which significantly improved the country's economic standing compared to previous years. He also raised Brazil's international profile, including securing a non-permanent seat on the UN Security Council.

### 15. 2009 — Creation of BRIC
In 2009, the BRIC group was formed, with Brazil joining as a founding member due to its position as South America's largest economy and its status as a leading developing nation. For several years after joining, Brazil experienced rapid GDP growth, reaching up to 8% in 2011. Today, BRICS remains an important geopolitical alliance with numerous ongoing initiatives.

### 16. 2014 — World Cup in Brazil
The 20th edition of the FIFA World Cup was hosted by Brazil from June 12 to July 13, 2014, with the final held in Rio de Janeiro (and ultimately won by Germany/Argentina in final stage). It was a major event for the country given football's central place in Brazilian culture, though Brazil did not win the tournament.

### 17. 2016 — Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro
Brazil hosted the 2016 Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro, amid criticism regarding the human rights conditions of residents in favelas (Brazil's informal settlements). Human rights NGOs, such as Amnesty International, accused security forces and military police of using excessive and unlawful force during security operations.

### 18. 2018 — Imprisonment of Lula
On April 7, Lula was incarcerated following an appeal decision by Judge Sérgio Moro after being convicted of receiving property from Petrobras in exchange for political favors. In 2019, he was released after the Supreme Court determined that Moro had colluded with prosecutors, compromising impartiality and preventing Lula's candidacy in the 2018 elections. Following Bolsonaro's inauguration, Moro was appointed Minister of Justice.

### 19. 2024 — Floods in Rio Grande do Sul
From April 26 to May 2, torrential rains driven by El Niño struck the southern state of Rio Grande do Sul, causing massive flooding. The Guaíba River reached a historic peak of 5.3 meters. The disaster left 107 people dead, 136 missing, 374 injured, and forced the evacuation of 150,000 residents, affecting over 1.47 million people overall.

### 20. 2025 — Imprisonment of Bolsonaro
Bolsonaro attempted a coup against his opponent, Lula, and was found guilty in September of leading a criminal organization that conspired to maintain power after Lula's 2022 election victory. He was sentenced to 27 years in prison. Donald Trump subsequently imposed tariffs on Brazil in an attempt to pressure the government into granting Bolsonaro a pardon.
